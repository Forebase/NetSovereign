# Domain foundation

An **institution** is a recognised actor; an **authority** is its power to declare, allocate, certify, route, admit, revoke, delegate, expose, mirror, or federate resources in scope. An authority is not CoreDNS, step-ca, Keycloak, PostgreSQL, nftables, or any service. A **mandate** bounds an authority's actions, resource classes, jurisdiction, validity, and constraints. Protocol surfaces may later materialise this meaning but are not its source.

World intent is canonical. Capabilities describe needs. Provider bindings are bounded, replaceable choices. Observed state and reconciliation do not exist in v0.1. In v0.2, a versioned observed-state snapshot is provider-neutral evidence supplied to an offline, read-only admission decision; it cannot amend intent, create authority, or trigger convergence. Missing, stale, contradictory, or unverifiable evidence is indeterminate rather than silently successful. Real-internet posture composes independently with cross-world posture. Peering imports suffix reachability/discovery, never trust. Federation declarations selectively import/export an explicit authority surface; federation execution and PKI cross-signing are not implemented.

Implemented in v0.1: strict parsing, semantic diagnostics, defaults, resolver derivation, manifest/graph generation, and explanations. The required sequence is v0.2 observation/admission, v0.3 provider-neutral compilation, then v0.4 reconciliation and durable operations. Operational DNS, PKI, identity, and gateway providers must not begin before that sequence is complete. Every server, network, durable store, provider execution, and security operation remains deferred to its version in the roadmap.
